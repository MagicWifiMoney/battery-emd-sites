Runtimes assume about 12% inverter losses and typical duty cycles. The honest summary: one module carries a home's essentials through the night or a workday outage, and pairs naturally with solar — a modest array refills 5kWh by early afternoon, which is why this size is the standard first step for off-grid cabins and RV-adjacent builds too.

'5kWh battery' can mean three different products

  • One 48V 100Ah module (this page): bare storage — needs a 48V inverter, wins on $/Wh, expands in matching steps.
  • A wall-mounted 5kWh unit: the same 5.12kWh in a slim finished case (several are in the table below); tidier, usually a small $/Wh premium.
  • A ~5kWh portable power station: battery, inverter and outlets in one box. A different purchase — the convenience is priced into its $/Wh, and it does not parallel into a home bank the way modules do. This tracker prices the module class; compare a station's $/Wh against the table before assuming it is the simpler buy.

Nearly every listing in the table is the 100Ah class wearing different cases — rack, wall and floor formats of the same 5.12kWh. The format decision (our rack-module siblings are tracked in depth at serverrackbatteries.com) matters more than the brand decision at this size; the $/Wh column prices whatever is left.

When 5kWh is enough — and the day it isn't

Enough: overnight essentials backup, a cabin or workshop on solar, a home office that cannot go down, the first module of a planned bank. Not enough: multi-day outages without recharge, electric heating or cooking as backup loads, or a well pump plus everything else at once — those start at 10kWh and usually land at 20.

Expanding later is a wiring job, not a replacement: same brand and model, in parallel, within the model's parallel limit (commonly up to 16). The series & parallel guide covers the rules, and the home battery system page compares the grown bank against installed-system quotes.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a 5kWh battery cost?

In the 48V module class, 5.12kWh of storage currently runs $799 at the cheapest tracked $/Wh to $1,126 at the median — the 22 listings in the table span $800–$1,699 per unit. Packaged and installed 5kWh systems cost more per kWh; the table is the live evidence.

Is a 5kWh battery the same as a 48V 100Ah battery?

Yes, in this class: 51.2V × 100Ah = 5,120Wh = 5.12kWh. '5kWh battery' and '48V 100Ah' are two searches for the same shelf of products, which is why the table on this page and the 100Ah table overlap almost completely.

How long will a 5kWh battery power a house?

Essentials only (fridge, lights, internet, ~300W average): roughly 15 hours. Add a furnace blower and it is about 7–8 hours. It will not carry electric heating, cooking or AC for long — those loads need a 10–20kWh bank.

Can I start with 5kWh and add more later?

Yes — that is the design. Modules of the same brand and model connect in parallel up to the model's limit, so a bank grows 5.12kWh at a time without replacing the first module. Buy the second module from the same model line while it is current.
