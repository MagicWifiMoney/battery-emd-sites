The two ways to buy the same kWh

Every home battery system is three things: battery cells in a box, an inverter that speaks to the grid and your panels, and the labor/permits to connect them. Installed brands bundle all three into one quote, which is why their per-kWh price is high: roundups of installed systems in 2026 cite $10,000–$25,000 all-in for roughly 10–13.5kWh of storage — about $750–$1,900 per kWh installed.

The DIY path unbundles it. You buy the batteries on this page (at the live $/kWh in the table), add a hybrid inverter (a planning estimate of $900–$2,500 for a 5–8kW unit, not a price we track), plus rack, cable and an electrician's day if you don't self-install. At today's median of $0.221/Wh, the battery portion of a 10.24kWh system is $2,263 — the single biggest line item is also the one with the live price on it. Most arguments about home battery pricing are really arguments about which layer a number belongs to, so here are the two layers stated separately. The installed layer is everything the hardware figure deliberately excludes: a hybrid inverter (a planning estimate of $900–$2,500 for a 5–8kW unit, not a price we track), the transfer equipment that connects the bank to the house, the labour to fit it, and the permits and inspection a grid-tied system needs. An installed quote bundles both layers into one number — current buying guides cite $10,000–$25,000 all-in for roughly 10–13.5kWh — which is why it cannot be compared against a battery-only price directly. Compare like with like: either strip an installed quote down to its battery line, or add the four installed-layer items to the hardware figure above before judging the gap.

What the extra money buys in an installed system

  • Permits and inspection handled by the installer — required for grid-tied systems in most US jurisdictions either way.
  • One warranty throat to choke: a single 10-year warranty vs separate battery/inverter warranties.
  • Whole-home automatic transfer with a fitted panel, vs the critical-loads sub-panel most DIY builds use.
  • No homework: no inverter-compatibility lists, no cable sizing, no settings.

None of that changes the storage math. A kWh from a rack module at $0.17–$0.23/Wh stores the same energy as a kWh in a $1,000/kWh installed box; the difference is labor, integration and warranty structure. Buyers who are comfortable speccing an inverter pay roughly a third to a half for the same backup.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a home battery system cost in 2026?

Installed systems are quoted at $10,000–$25,000 in current buying guides for about 10–13.5kWh. A DIY system built from 48V LiFePO4 batteries prices its storage live on this page — recently a median of $0.221/Wh, so $2,263 for 10.24kWh of batteries — plus a hybrid inverter (roughly $900–$2,500) and any electrical labor.

Is a DIY home battery system legal to grid-tie?

Grid-tied systems generally need permits, listed equipment and inspection wherever you live — the DIY/installed distinction is about who does the paperwork, not whether it exists. Off-grid and backup-only systems behind a transfer switch face fewer requirements in many areas, but local code always wins; check before you build.

How long will a 10kWh home battery system run a house?

It depends entirely on what stays on. Essentials (fridge, lights, internet, ~300W average) run roughly 30 hours on 10.24kWh. Add a furnace blower and usage climbs; run an electric range or AC and the same bank lasts a few hours. Size from loads, not from the house's square footage.
