Price per Watt-Hour, Explained with Live Numbers

Nobody searches for 'price per watt hour' — the phrase measures near zero volume, and this page exists anyway, because $/Wh is the method behind every table on this site. Sticker prices can't be compared across battery sizes; amp-hours can't be compared across voltages. Price per watt-hour fixes both. Right now, across 37 tracked 48V models, stored energy runs from $0.150/Wh to $0.909/Wh — a spread most shoppers never see because no maker publishes it against its competitors.

Prices below come from the manufacturers' own stores and were last refreshed November 22, 2026. We rank by price per watt-hour ($/Wh) — the fairest way to compare batteries of different sizes. Listings are data, not reviews: we have not hands-on tested these batteries.

The worked example (today's live cheapest)

The current $/Wh leader in the table below is the ECO-WORTHY PowerMega 314 | 51.2V 314AH Lithium Battery 16kWh | Active Balance | UL1973 — 1 Pack at $2,400. Its capacity is 312Ah · 16,000Wh, so the math is $2,400 ÷ its watt-hours = $0.150/Wh. Do the same division for any battery — price ÷ (volts × amp-hours) — and every product in this category lands on one comparable scale, whatever its size or shape.

When $/Wh is not the whole story

  • Features cost money: self-heating, Bluetooth monitoring and inverter communications raise $/Wh — sometimes worth it (a frozen battery that can't charge is worth $0/Wh).
  • Form factor: rack modules are cheap per Wh but need a rack and a 48V inverter; a drop-in 12V costs more per Wh and installs in minutes.
  • Warranty and support: a premium brand at $0.30/Wh with a 10-year US warranty can be the rational buy over a $0.15/Wh no-name — $/Wh ranks price, not risk.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate price per watt-hour?

Divide the price by the battery's watt-hours. Watt-hours = nominal volts × amp-hours (a 51.2V 100Ah battery = 5,120Wh). A $860 battery at 5,120Wh costs $0.168/Wh. Multiply by 1,000 for $/kWh ($168/kWh).

What is a good price per watt-hour for LiFePO4?

It moves with the market, which is why this site tracks it live: the 48V table currently spans $0.150–$0.909/Wh with a $0.238 median. As a orientation, bare batteries and rack modules sit at the low end; small, feature-heavy and premium-brand packs sit at the high end.

Why don't manufacturers advertise $/Wh?

Because it makes premium pricing visible. A brand whose battery costs twice the $/Wh of a competitor's prefers to sell capacity stories, cycle counts and features. A price-per-unit table is the one comparison no single maker will publish against the whole market — so we do.

How prices are gathered: automatically from the manufacturers' own online stores, refreshed several times a day. We compute price per watt-hour from each battery's rated capacity.
