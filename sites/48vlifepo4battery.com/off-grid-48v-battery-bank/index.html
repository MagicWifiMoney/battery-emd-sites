Building an Off-Grid 48V Battery Bank That Survives November

An off-grid bank is not a backup battery — it is the power company. Sizing flips accordingly: you start from daily consumption (not outage loads) and multiply by autonomy days (how many sunless days the bank must bridge). A frugal cabin at 5kWh/day with 2 days of autonomy needs 10kWh usable — call it 12kWh of nameplate — while a full homestead at 20kWh/day needs four times that. Nameplate includes the two off-grid realities: you plan to use ~80% of rated capacity day-to-day (LiFePO4 allows it, but headroom extends life), and autonomy is calculated on usable energy. The other half of the design is generation: the array must refill a day's use in a winter day's sun, or the biggest bank only delays the generator.

Why 48V is the off-grid default

  • Current stays sane: a 5kW load draws ~100A at 48V vs ~400A at 12V — thinner cables, smaller breakers, less heat.
  • Inverter selection: the serious off-grid inverter-chargers (and every hybrid worth its warranty) are 48V machines.
  • Daily cycling economics: at one cycle a day, cycle life is calendar life — a 6,000-cycle bank is a 16-year asset, which is why the $/Wh you pay for it matters more off-grid than anywhere else. The table's sort is the whole argument.

Frequently asked questions

How big should an off-grid battery bank be?

Daily consumption × autonomy days ÷ 0.8 (usable share). A home using 10kWh/day that wants 2 sunless days needs ~25kWh nameplate — five 5.12kWh modules. Measure or honestly estimate daily kWh first; it is the number everything else multiplies.

How long does an off-grid LiFePO4 bank last?

At one cycle per day, a bank rated 4,000–6,000 cycles reaches its cycle rating in 11–16 years, and LiFePO4 typically continues at reduced capacity beyond it. Calendar aging, heat and chronic undercharging from an undersized array are the usual real-world limiters.

Can I add modules to an off-grid bank later?

Yes, within the model's parallel limit and ideally within the first year or two, matching brand and model. Off-grid banks commonly grow in stages — array first, bank second, or the reverse — as budget allows.
