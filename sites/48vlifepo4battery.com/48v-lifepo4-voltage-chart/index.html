48V LiFePO4 Voltage Chart: What Each Reading Means for State of Charge

A rested 48V LiFePO4 battery reads about 54.4V when full, about 52.2V at half and 40.0V when empty, per Renogy's published chart for a 16-cell pack. Two things surprise people: the middle of the curve is flat (70% to 30% spans only 1.3V), and a reading of 48V means about 10% left, not a healthy nominal battery. Per-cell figures are Renogy's; pack figures are the cell figure times the number of cells in series, as Renogy lists them. Makers' charts differ by a few hundredths of a volt per cell, so treat any row as a band, not a decimal-point reading.

"48V" and "51.2V" batteries are the same 16-cell pack; 51.2V is its true nominal voltage and 48V is the system class it plugs into. Server rack modules, wall batteries and golf cart packs all follow this chart. LiTime's chart (November 2026) draws the top a little lower, with a full cell resting around 3.35V, about 53.6V for the pack, so a just-charged battery resting at 53.5V to 54.4V is full. The charge settings are on our 48V charging guide.

Why 48V on the meter means nearly empty

On the chart, the nameplate 51.2V is the 20% row and 48.0V is the 10% row. Anyone used to lead-acid, where 48V reads as a normal mid-charge bank, will misread a LiFePO4 pack sitting at 48V as fine when it has about a tenth of its energy left. Below that the curve falls fast toward 40.0V, which is where voltage becomes a useful warning.

Set the inverter's low-voltage cutoff (or the golf cart controller's) near the 10% row so the equipment stops before the battery's BMS has to, and recharge promptly after a deep discharge.

Why the middle is flat, and what to read instead

Between 70% (52.8V) and 30% (51.5V) the pack moves only 1.3V, about 0.08V per cell. Meter error, temperature or a load switched off a minute earlier can move a reading that much. For a real percentage, read the battery's own state of charge: a Bluetooth model's app, a shunt monitor, or, on rack modules talking to a hybrid inverter over CAN or RS485, the inverter's display, which then shows the BMS's counted figure instead of guessing from voltage. Our sister tracker covers that closed-loop setup on its inverter compatibility page.

Reading it correctly: rest, load, charge, and parallel banks

  • Rested means no charge and no load. Disconnect both and let the bank sit before reading.
  • On the charger, the meter reads the charger. Expect up to about 58.4V while charging; that is the setting, not the state of charge.
  • Under load, every battery reads low. A 5kW inverter load or a golf cart climbing a hill sags a full pack well below 54V.
  • In a parallel bank, every module reads the same. Modules tied together share one voltage, so check each module's own app or display (or read each one before you connect them) to spot a weak unit.

Frequently asked questions

What voltage is a fully charged 48V LiFePO4 battery?

About 54.4V at rest and 58.4V while still on the charger, per Renogy's published chart for a 16-cell pack. LiTime's chart puts a full, rested pack a little lower, around 53.3V to 53.6V, so a just-charged battery resting anywhere in that range is full.

What voltage is 50% on a 48V LiFePO4 battery?

About 52.2V at rest, per Renogy's chart, with 60% at 52.3V and 40% at 52.0V. Voltage can't separate mid-range charge levels on LiFePO4; the BMS's own state-of-charge reading, through an app or a closed-loop inverter, can.

Is 48 volts low for a 48V lithium battery?

Yes. On a rested 16-cell LiFePO4 pack, 48.0V is about 10% charge and the nameplate 51.2V is about 20%, per Renogy's chart. Empty is 40.0V. Recharge soon, and set your inverter or controller to cut off around 48V.
