Charging a 48V LiFePO4 Battery: The 58.4V Rule

A '48V' LiFePO4 battery is a 51.2V pack, and it charges at 58.4V absorption (16 cells × 3.65V) — that single number, plus 'no equalization, ever,' is most of this guide. Whether the voltage comes from an inverter-charger, a solar controller or a bench charger matters less than the profile being a lithium one. What follows is the settings table and the failure modes.

If your battery talks to your inverter (CAN/RS485 closed-loop), the BMS sets these values itself and the table becomes a cross-check rather than an input — one reason rack modules, which almost all have comms, are forgiving in inverter systems. The rack-format page covers that class.

The three charging mistakes that actually hurt

  • The lead-acid charger with equalization: a 48V golf-cart or forklift charger that runs an automatic equalize cycle will push the pack over-voltage until the BMS disconnects — repeatedly, every charge. If the charger can't disable equalization, it can't charge lithium.
  • Charging below freezing: lithium plates permanently when charged under 0°C. Unheated garage installs need a BMS with low-temp cutoff (verify it — not all have it) or a self-heating model.
  • Bank charging with mixed states of charge: paralleling a full module with an empty one creates a large equalizing current between them. Bring modules to a similar voltage before connecting, and charge the bank as one battery thereafter.

Charging from solar follows the same profile through the MPPT's lithium setting; charging from the grid or a generator is the inverter-charger's job. Either way the battery only cares about the voltage curve and the temperature — see the batteries themselves, ranked by $/Wh, on the 48V table.

Frequently asked questions

What voltage should I charge a 48V LiFePO4 battery to?

58.4V for a 51.2V (16-cell) pack — some manufacturers specify a 56.8–58.4V window, and their manual wins. Never use a charger whose profile exceeds 58.4V or runs an equalization stage.

Can I use my 48V lead-acid golf cart charger on LiFePO4?

Only if it has a lithium profile or its equalization can be fully disabled and its output capped at 58.4V. Most basic lead-acid cart chargers fail one of those tests; a LiFePO4-profile 58.4V charger is the correct replacement.

How long does a 48V 100Ah battery take to charge?

At a 50A charge rate, about 2 hours from empty plus a short absorption tail; at 20A, about 5 hours. In inverter systems the practical limit is usually the inverter-charger's charge rating or the solar array, not the battery.
