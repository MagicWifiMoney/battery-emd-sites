How Long a 12V 100Ah Battery Runs a Fridge (It's the Fridge's Fault)

The battery is the constant — 1,280Wh, of which about 980Wh reaches a home fridge after inverter losses. The fridge is the variable: a modern full-size fridge eats 1.0–1.5kWh a day (answer: most of a day), while a 12V compressor fridge sips 0.35–0.6kWh a day (answer: 2–3 days). Daily-energy figures are typical consumption ranges, not measurements of your fridge — the next section shows how to get your number. Home-fridge runtimes assume a pure-sine inverter at ~85% efficiency and a battery used to ~90% depth, which LiFePO4 tolerates daily. The usable-capacity gap behind those runtimes is also what decides the LiFePO4 vs lead-acid cost comparison: lead-acid's 50% rule halves the same nameplate before the duty cycle even starts.

Why the nameplate watts lie (duty cycle)

A fridge rated '150W' does not draw 150W all day — its compressor cycles, typically running 30–50% of the time in a cool room and 60%+ in a hot garage. Runtime math must use average watts: nameplate × duty cycle. 150W × 40% = 60W average = 1.44kWh/day, which is why the home-fridge row above lands where it does. The reliable way to know yours: a plug-in energy meter (Kill A Watt style) over 48 hours gives you the exact kWh/day — a $20 measurement that replaces every estimate on this page. The checks that apply to the battery itself — group size, low-temperature protection, series rating — are the buying guide's checklist.

Two levers extend runtime more than any battery upgrade: keep the fridge out of the heat (duty cycle falls), and skip the inverter where you can — a 12V compressor fridge runs straight off the battery, which is why its row laps the field. If the goal is multi-day fridge backup, the 200Ah class doubles every number in the table at a lower $/Wh. The same duty-cycle math sizes this site's other two runtime questions: a CPAP's nights per charge are set by its heaters the way a fridge's hours are set by its compressor, and a van's battery audit starts from this same fridge row.

Frequently asked questions

How long will a 100Ah LiFePO4 run a 12V fridge?

A 12V compressor fridge uses about 0.35–0.6kWh per day, and a 100Ah LiFePO4 delivers ~1,150Wh DC without inverter losses — so roughly 2 to 3 days, depending on ambient temperature and how often the lid opens.

Will a 12V 100Ah battery run a full-size fridge overnight?

Yes, with margin: overnight (8–10 hours) a modern home fridge needs roughly 0.4–0.6kWh, and the battery delivers ~0.98kWh through an inverter. A full day is the realistic ceiling; two days needs a second battery or the 200Ah class.

Does the inverter waste a lot of battery?

About 15%: conversion losses plus the inverter's own idle draw (10–25W just being on — up to 0.5kWh/day if left idling, which is why the runtime table assumes sensible use). A DC fridge avoids both penalties entirely.
