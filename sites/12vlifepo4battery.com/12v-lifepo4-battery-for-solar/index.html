Undersize the panels and a big bank never fills (a LiFePO4 tolerates that, but you paid for capacity you can't fill); oversize them and a small bank is full by 10am while harvest is thrown away. Daily cycling is also where LiFePO4's cycle life pays: at one cycle a day, a 4,000-cycle rating is a decade of service — the arithmetic is priced against lead-acid in the cost-per-delivered-kWh comparison. Solar-specific checks

  • Charge controller profile: your MPPT must have a lithium/LiFePO4 profile (14.2–14.6V absorption, no equalization). A lead-acid-only controller undercharges and can damage the BMS over time.
  • Low-temp protection: an unheated shed or cabin install needs a BMS that blocks sub-freezing charging — solar charges whenever the sun shines, including at -5°F.
  • Idle draw: a battery that sits in a cabin system all winter should have a true off state or very low self-discharge; LiFePO4's ~1–3%/month is one of its quiet advantages over lead-acid.

Frequently asked questions

How many solar panels do I need for a 12V 100Ah LiFePO4 battery?

Roughly 200–400W of panel. At 300W and 4–5 good sun-hours, you harvest about 1.2–1.5kWh on a clear day — enough to refill a 1,280Wh battery from empty while covering light daytime loads. More panel helps in winter and cloudy climates.

Can a 12V LiFePO4 battery sit at partial charge?

Yes — unlike lead-acid, LiFePO4 is comfortable at partial state of charge and does not sulfate. Storing it around 50–80% and cycling daily is normal solar service; avoid only the extremes of sitting at 100% for months in high heat.

Do I need a special charge controller for LiFePO4 solar?

You need one with a LiFePO4/lithium profile: 14.2–14.6V absorption, no equalization stage, and ideally a low-temperature charge cutoff paired with the battery's own BMS protection. Most modern MPPT controllers include the profile.
