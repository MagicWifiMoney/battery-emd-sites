Two different bets on the same battery

LiTime's bet is price per watt-hour. Its 100Ah line spans bare-bones packs through Bluetooth and low-temperature variants, and its cheapest rows anchor the bottom of this table. The catalog is wide because the strategy is: a LiTime at every feature tier, each priced to be the $/Wh problem for everyone else.

Renogy's bet is the system around the battery. Renogy sells panels, charge controllers, inverters and monitoring as one ecosystem, and its battery prices carry that premium — its Pro and smart lines add self-heating and app monitoring, and its retail presence (including marketplace storefronts) is broader. Buyers already inside the Renogy ecosystem pay for one support door and matched components.

Reading the table like a buyer

  • Same energy, every row: all models here are ~1,280Wh, so $/Wh is a pure price comparison — no capacity excuses.
  • Feature tiers explain the spread: within each brand, the jump from the cheapest row buys Bluetooth, low-temp protection or self-heating. Decide which features you need before comparing stickers across brands.
  • Depth is a tiebreaker: 17 vs 7 tracked models means both brands will have a variant that fits your box and your charger; neither leaves you stranded on sizing.

Frequently asked questions

Is LiTime or Renogy cheaper for 12V 100Ah?

On live maker-store prices, LiTime's cheapest 100Ah-class model is $190 ($0.148/Wh) against Renogy's $308 ($0.241/Wh). Renogy's premium buys its ecosystem and smart features; the table shows the current gap rather than a remembered one.

Are LiTime and Renogy batteries the same capacity?

In this class, yes: both brands' 100Ah models store 1,280Wh (12.8V × 100Ah). Differences are BMS features (Bluetooth, low-temperature protection, self-heating), case size, warranty terms and price — not energy.

Can I mix LiTime and Renogy batteries in one bank?

Manufacturers advise against mixing brands, models or ages in a series/parallel bank — BMS behaviors differ and the weakest unit limits the string. Pick one brand per bank; the price difference is rarely worth the imbalance risk.
