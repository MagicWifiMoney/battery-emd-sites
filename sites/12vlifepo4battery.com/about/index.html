About this tracker

This site exists for one reason: batteries are sold by amp-hours and brand stories, but the fair way to compare them is price per watt-hour ($/Wh) — what each unit of stored energy actually costs. A $449 battery with 4,019Wh ($0.112/Wh) is cheaper energy than a $300 battery with 1,280Wh ($0.234/Wh), even though the sticker says otherwise.

Method

  • Prices are collected automatically from the manufacturers' own online stores, several times a day.
  • Capacity comes from each product's rated voltage × amp-hours (or the maker's stated watt-hours). Where a feed is ambiguous, the listing is excluded rather than guessed.
  • $/Wh = price ÷ watt-hours. $/kWh is the same number × 1,000.
  • We do not hands-on test batteries, and we never publish invented ratings, invented discounts or fake "was" prices. A struck-through price appears only when the maker's own store lists one.
  • If a price feed is more than 72 hours old, this site hides the numbers rather than show you stale prices.

Some outbound links are affiliate links: if you buy through them, the site may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. That is how the tracker pays for itself. Rankings are computed from price data only — no brand can pay for placement, and the sort order is the same math for everyone.

Corrections

Spot a wrong capacity or a dead link? The data refreshes several times a day, so most errors fix themselves on the next pass. Persistent errors usually trace back to the maker's own product feed.
