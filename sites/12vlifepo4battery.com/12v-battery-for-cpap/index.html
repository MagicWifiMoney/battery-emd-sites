A 12V Battery for CPAP Camping: The Heater Decides Your Nights

The CPAP motor is not the load — the heaters are. Run therapy-only (humidifier off, standard tube) and a typical machine averages roughly 15–25W, so a 12V 100Ah battery's ~970Wh of delivered energy lasts about 5–8 nights. Turn the heated humidifier and heated tube up and the same machine can draw 60–90W — the same battery is a 1–2 night battery. Use the DC converter, not an inverter

Almost every CPAP is a DC machine wearing an AC brick: the therapy unit itself runs on low-voltage DC, and the maker sells a 12V DC converter for vehicle and battery use. Powering it through an AC inverter instead pays three taxes: conversion loss on the way up to AC and back down, the inverter's own idle draw (commonly 10–25W — over an 8-hour night that is 80–200Wh, up to a full therapy-only night, spent powering nothing), and one more box to pack. The DC converter route is why the table above can use a ~970Wh delivered figure at all.

  • Buy the maker's converter for your exact model — CPAP DC inputs are model-specific (voltage and connector), and a generic adapter that under-volts the machine can fault it mid-night.
  • Turn the heaters down before you buy a bigger battery: dropping from heated-tube-max to therapy-only is worth more nights than doubling the battery, and it is free. A passive (water-filled, unheated) humidifier is the middle setting many campers settle on.
  • Test the whole rig at home for a full night first, at the settings you will actually use, and note the battery's state of charge in the morning. That one rehearsal replaces every estimate on this page with your number — do not let the first real test be the trip.

Sizing the trip, honestly

Work backwards from nights × your row in the table. A weekend (2–3 nights) at therapy-only or modest humidity fits inside one 100Ah battery with reserve — the class in the table below, where the same 1,280Wh in every row makes $/Wh a pure price ranking. A week with the humidifier running is a 200Ah conversation, or a 100Ah plus a way to recharge (vehicle DC-DC on driving days, or a folding panel at camp — the solar page prices that pairing). And a note that outranks the math: if your therapy is not optional, carry the reserve — size for the worst row you might actually run, not the best row you hope to.

Frequently asked questions

How long will a 12V 100Ah battery run a CPAP?

It depends almost entirely on the heaters. Therapy-only (humidifier off), a typical machine averages 15–25W and a 100Ah battery delivers roughly 5–8 eight-hour nights. With a heated humidifier, plan on 2–3 nights; with humidifier and heated tube at high settings, 1–2 nights. The maker's 12V DC converter (not an AC inverter) is assumed in all three figures.

Can I run my CPAP straight off a 12V battery?

Not straight — through the machine maker's 12V DC converter for your exact model. CPAPs run on low-voltage DC internally but at model-specific voltages and connectors; the official converter steps the battery's 12V to what the machine expects. Never improvise the connection with a generic adapter.

Is a 100Ah battery enough for a week of CPAP camping?

At therapy-only settings, yes — 5–8 nights covers most weeks, with the top of the range needing the lower-draw end. With a heated humidifier every night, no: a week needs roughly 2,000–2,800Wh delivered, which means the 200Ah class, a second 100Ah, or a recharge source during the trip.
