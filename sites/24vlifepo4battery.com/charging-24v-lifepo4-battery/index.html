Charging a 24V LiFePO4 Battery, Correctly

A 24V LiFePO4 battery (25.6V nominal, eight cells) wants one thing from a charger: a lithium profile that tops out at 29.2V and never runs an equalization stage. Get that right and the battery charges fully and lasts its rated cycles; get it wrong and the usual symptom is a pack that quietly stops at 80%. EqualizationOFF, alwaysEqualization voltages overcharge lithium and trip the BMS
Charge current0.2C–0.5C typical20–50A for a 100Ah pack; the maker's max charge current is the ceiling
Low-temperature chargingNot below 0°C / 32°FUnless the pack is self-heating; protected BMS units refuse the charge

Why 29.2V: each LiFePO4 cell charges to 3.65V, and eight in series is 29.2V. A charger that stops at lead-acid 24V settings (about 28.8V absorption is close, but gel/AGM profiles with temperature compensation and equalization are not) leaves capacity on the table or abuses the pack — the lithium profile exists precisely to avoid both.

The four ways a 24V pack gets charged

  • Dedicated 24V LiFePO4 charger (shore power): the simplest correct answer for boats and garages. Pick one with a lithium profile and a current rating near 0.2–0.5C of your pack.
  • Solar MPPT controller: set battery type to lithium/user and enter the voltages above; the controller must be rated for a 24V battery bank (most mid-size MPPTs auto-detect 12/24V — confirm before wiring panels).
  • DC-DC charger from a vehicle: trucks and RVs charging from an alternator need a 24V-output DC-DC unit with a lithium profile; a bare alternator connection is not a charge profile.
  • Onboard marine charger: multi-bank chargers charge each bank at its own voltage — a 24V trolling bank needs a 24V bank output, not a tap across a series pair.

The series-pair trap

If your '24V battery' is actually two 12V batteries in series (see the series vs native guide), charging has an extra rule: charge the pair as a 24V bank with a 24V lithium charger, or charge each 12V battery individually — but do not leave one charger on one battery of a working pair, which unbalances them. A battery balancer across the pair keeps the two halves at the same state of charge over time. Native 24V packs sidestep all of this: one case, one BMS, one 29.2V charge.

Storage, briefly: LiFePO4 stores best near 50–70% charge, disconnected or with loads off, and self-discharges slowly. A pack put away full in a hot space ages fastest; a pack put away at half charge in a cool one barely notices the season.

Frequently asked questions

Can I charge a 24V LiFePO4 battery with a 12V charger?

No. A 12V charger tops out around 14.6V — half of what a 25.6V pack needs. You need a 24V charger with a lithium profile (29.2V absorption), an MPPT controller set for a 24V lithium bank, or a 24V DC-DC charger.

What voltage should I charge my 24V LiFePO4 battery to?

29.2V absorption (3.65V per cell × 8) is the standard full-charge setting; some makers specify 28.8V. Float is optional at about 27.2V or off entirely, and equalization must be disabled.

Will my old lead-acid charger work on a 24V lithium battery?

Only if it has a true lithium profile. Lead-acid profiles with equalization or aggressive temperature compensation can overcharge lithium and trip the BMS; an absorption near 28.8–29.2V with no equalization is the requirement, and most basic lead-acid chargers cannot guarantee it.
