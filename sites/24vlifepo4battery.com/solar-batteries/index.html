Solar is the daily-cycling job: the bank charges from the panels by day and carries the loads at night, so cycle life and cost per watt-hour matter more than peak specs. The packs below are the 24V LiFePO4 models the makers sell for solar and off-grid use, ranked by $/Wh.

When is 24V the right solar bus voltage? In the middle: a 24V bank pairs with the 1–3kW inverter class that runs cabins, workshops, larger RVs and small off-grid homes — at 2,000W a 24V system draws about half the current of a 12V one (~78A vs ~156A), so cables and losses stay sane, while the bank stays one or two batteries instead of a rack. Below that class, 12V is simpler; above it, 48V takes over. Building the bank from two 12V packs in series instead of a native 24V battery is the other real option — our series vs native 24V guide walks through the trade-offs, and the 24V buying guide covers sizing from your daily loads.
