24V Two Ways: One Native Battery, or Two 12V Batteries in Series?

Every 24V system starts with this fork: buy a native 24V battery (eight cells, one BMS, one case) or wire two 12V batteries in series (the amp-hours stay the same, the voltage doubles). Both are legitimate; they differ in balance management, charging discipline and failure behavior. What series wiring actually does

Two 12V 100Ah batteries in series make a 24V 100Ah battery: 25.6V nominal, 2,560Wh — identical energy to the native packs in the table. Amp-hours do not add in series (they add in parallel). The catch is that you now own two BMS units and two cell groups that must stay at the same state of charge. If one half drifts — and halves always drift a little — the weaker half limits the whole bank and ages faster, which widens the drift. A battery balancer (a small device wired across the pair) bleeds charge from the fuller half to the emptier one and is close to mandatory for a series pair you intend to keep for years.

The cost comparison, and a verdict by situation

Price the two paths in $/Wh, not stickers. The native 100Ah class above currently starts at $0.156/Wh (Eco-Worthy); the 12V 100Ah class on 12vlifepo4battery.com usually prices a little lower per Wh because it is the highest-volume class in the industry — two of them plus a balancer can undercut the native pack, or not, depending on the week's prices. Run both tables before deciding; the gap moves.

  • New 24V trolling or inverter install, nothing owned yet: native. One BMS, one charger, no balancer, no drift — the simple system wins.
  • You already own one good 12V battery: series, with a matched twin (same brand, model and age) and a balancer. Reusing a paid-for battery beats any $/Wh gap.
  • Weight or space is split across two lockers: series — two 25 lb cases go where one 50 lb case cannot.
  • Planning to grow to 48V later: neither is a dead end, but price the jump now — four 12V in series is a common path, while native 24V packs do not series into most 48V systems.

Frequently asked questions

Do amp-hours add when batteries are in series?

No. Series adds voltage, not amp-hours: two 12V 100Ah batteries in series are a 24V 100Ah battery storing 2,560Wh. Parallel wiring is the opposite — voltage stays, amp-hours add.

Can any two 12V LiFePO4 batteries be wired in series for 24V?

No — the model must explicitly allow series connection (some budget packs are parallel-only), and the pair should be the same brand, model, capacity and age, charged to the same state before wiring. A battery balancer across the pair is strongly recommended.

Is a native 24V battery more expensive than two 12V batteries?

Sometimes. The 12V 100Ah class is the industry's highest-volume size and often prices lower per watt-hour; two of them plus a balancer can beat a native 24V pack, or lose to it, depending on current prices.
